From the misty mountains of southern China to gardens around the world — explore the diversity and beauty of Chinese orchids.

Orchid Origins

China is one of the original homes of orchids. Wild orchids grow across the Yangtze River basin and southern mountain regions. For over two thousand years, the Chinese have cultivated and cherished these elegant plants.

Orchid Species

The main Chinese orchid species include: Cymbidium goeringii (Spring Orchid), C. ensifolium (Jian Orchid), C. faberi (Hui Orchid), C. sinense (Mo Orchid), and C. kanran (Cold Orchid). Each has its unique character and blooming season.

The Five Traditional Chinese Orchids

The five classic cymbidiums of China — each with its own season, character, and centuries of cultivated tradition.

Chunlan · Spring Orchid

Cymbidium goeringii

The most beloved Chinese orchid, blooming in early spring. Its delicate fragrance and elegant single blooms have made it the symbol of the orchid tradition.

Meet Chunlan →

Huilan · Noble Orchid

Cymbidium faberi

Tall and stately, blooming in late spring with many flowers per stem. Its upright bearing has earned it the name "noble orchid."

Meet Huilan →

Jianlan · Sword Orchid

Cymbidium ensifolium

Blooming in summer and autumn, often twice a year. Its sword-like leaves and resilient nature make it the most forgiving orchid for beginners.

Meet Jianlan →

Hanlan · Cold Orchid

Cymbidium kanran

The winter bloomer. Hanlan flowers open in the cold months with a pure, penetrating fragrance — a quiet treasure of the winter garden.

Meet Hanlan →

Molan · Ink Orchid

Cymbidium sinense

Dark glossy leaves and deep, richly colored blooms — the "ink orchid" of southern China, treasured for its bold beauty and New Year bloom.

Meet Molan →

Beyond the traditional Chinese cymbidiums — the spectacular orchids of the wider world, from tropical rainforests to high mountains.

The world's most popular houseplant orchid, with long-lasting arching sprays of butterfly-like blooms in every color.
